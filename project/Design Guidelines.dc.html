01

Brand & logo

The mark is three courses of brickwork in a rounded square: a full course, a stretcher bond with an offset joint, and a single orange course at the base. It stands for the job being built up in layers, and it reads at 16px.

Name & tagline. Always "Builder OS": two words, capital B, capital O and S. Never "BuilderOS" or "Builder O.S.". Primary line: Quotes that win the job, built in minutes. Descriptor: The operating system for UK renovation companies.
02

Colour

Warm, near-neutral greys with a single ink colour. Orange is our only brand accent. Use it like a highlighter, not a paint roller: for the selected cell, a live notification dot, one course of the logo. Never use it for large fills or primary buttons.

Dark sections
Marketing only. One or two per page for rhythm. Surface #111110, raised #1B1B19, border #262624, body text #A3A29C, headings #FFFFFF.
Contrast
Body text is Ink 2 (#5C5B57) or darker on light surfaces, which passes 4.5:1 on white. Muted #8A8984 is for metadata at 12px+ only, never for anything someone needs to act on.
03

Typography

Two families from Google Fonts. Geist for everything. Geist Mono for codes, references, URLs and section eyebrows. Headings are tight (negative tracking scales with size). Numbers in tables and totals always use tabular figures.

04

Space, radius & elevation

A 4px base grid. The app is dense (13px body, 32–36px controls). Marketing is airy (16–18px body, 112–128px between sections, 1200px max width). We draw edges with a 1px ring shadow instead of borders, so cards can sit edge to edge without doubling up.

06

Icons & imagery

Lucide only, at 1.5px stroke (the shadcn default). 14–16px in the app, 20px in marketing feature tiles. Icons sit next to a label. They never replace one, except in universally understood controls (search, close, more).

08

Voice & tone

We sound like a founder who's run jobs, writing to someone who's running one now: warm, plain-spoken and British. Confident without the hype. We know the trade's words (first fix, padstone, snagging) and we use them correctly.
